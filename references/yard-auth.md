# Yard Auth

Sign-in for projects: the Yard edge authenticates visitors and hands services trusted headers, so never build OAuth, sessions or password storage. The one screen a team builds is the sign-in page for project accounts (below), and yard runs everything behind it. Docs: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/yard-auth.md (access modes, headers, session endpoints, guests, products, external apps, consent, project accounts), https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/sign-in-page.md (the sign-in page: how visitors reach it, both calls, every answer and refusal, a whole page with the helpers and one with `fetch`, where it has to sit). Any access other than `public` needs `.team_permissions.yard_auth`.

## Access modes and headers

`access` on the service entry (services.md): `public` (everyone), `authenticated` (must sign in, with a yard account or a project account), `users` (people who hold one of the project's tiers, by a purchase, a subscription or a trial; a product bought on its own does not count, and neither does only having signed in, though the dashboard's Users list shows those people too), `team` (members of the owning team only, for internal tools). Whenever the visitor is signed in, whatever the mode, the request carries:

| Header | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `X-Yard-User-Id` | the id this project knows the user by, a lowercase UUID; store it as `TEXT` |
| `X-Yard-Email` | omitted when the account has none |
| `X-Yard-Entitlement` | `none`, `trial`, `active` or `team` |
| `X-Yard-Tier` / `X-Yard-Tier-Key` | the display name and the **key** of the tier they hold, sent with `trial` and `active`; gate features on the key |
| `X-Yard-Sandbox` | the sandbox name, empty for the project itself |
| `X-Yard-Guest-Id` | the browser's guest id, signed in or out, once it has one |

| Entitlement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | signed in, holding none of the project's tiers; a refunded purchase, a past-due or ended subscription and a product on its own all count as `none` |
| `trial` | a running free trial of a tier, including a subscription not yet charged |
| `active` | holds a tier, through a one-time purchase or a paid subscription |
| `team` | a member of the owning team, in any role, whatever they bought; carries no tier |

- The user id is per project: the same person has a different id on each project, even on two projects of one team, while emails and usernames are the same everywhere. Within one project it is the same in the project and every one of its sandboxes, and everything yard gives the project names the person by it (`__yard/auth/me`, `ownership()`, external apps' sign-in, webhooks, checkout's `for`, the users the dashboard, CLI, MCP tools and API list), so key data by it within the project and never use it to match people across projects (use the email for that, when the app has it). A team-wide users list has a row and an id for each project a person signed in to or bought, and the subscribers list one for each project they subscribed to. Local dev personas have fixed made-up ids (local-dev.md).
- No `X-Yard-User-Id` means an anonymous visitor (possible only on `public` services). Clients cannot forge the headers: incoming `X-Yard-*` is stripped, except a webhook delivery's `X-Yard-Signature` and `X-Yard-Event` (webhooks.md), which pass so a service can receive its project's webhooks.
- Every member of the owning team, in any role, is `team` everywhere once signed in and passes every gate, so the team never buys its own project. Signing in also gives their browser team access, which is what opens a private or draft project, a private sandbox and a `team` service: `logout` leaves it (a developer still sees their own draft project signed out), and signing out at yard.sh ends it and their sign-in on the project within a minute (anyone off the team stays signed in to the project). A `team` service always gets the team member's headers with `team`, even while they are signed out on the project; `authenticated` and `users` follow the visitor's sign-in alone, so a member who signed out on the project is asked to sign in again there, on a draft project too. Entitlement resolves team, else a paid subscription or purchase (`active`), else a running trial (`trial`), else `none`. A change of entitlement reaches the service within about 60 s; a long-lived UI polls `__yard/auth/me`.
- Gated requests are answered by the edge before your code runs. A navigation (`Sec-Fetch-Dest: document` or `iframe`, or `text/html` in `Accept`): signed out gets a `302` to sign in, signed in without a purchase on `users` a `302` to the sales page, and signed in outside the team on `team` a `403` "Team only" page. Signing in means the project's own sign-in page on an `authenticated` or `users` service, when project accounts are on and the release names a page in a public service (Project accounts below), and yard's sign-in, `__yard/auth/login`, otherwise; a draft, a private sandbox and a `team` service always use yard's. Anything else (`fetch`, curl, a WebSocket): `401 {"error_code": "sign_in_required"}`, `403 {"error_code": "purchase_required"}` or `403 {"error_code": "team_only"}`. So a page's `fetch` sees a lapsed session as a `401`: reload the page, which goes where the gate sends signed-out visitors, or send the browser to `__yard/auth/login?return=<path>` for Sign in with yard.

## `__yard/*` endpoints

Answered by the edge at the project root (`/<slug>/__yard/...`, where the landing page, the service at `/`, reaches them) and under every other service (`/<slug>/<service>/__yard/...`), nowhere deeper. Call them with relative URLs from a page at that level (`../__yard/auth/me` from a subfolder).

- `login?return=<path>`: signs the visitor in with yard (an existing Yard session passes silently) and redirects to `return`, a path relative to where login was called (`/` is the service's root, or the landing page at the project root; anything not starting with `/` falls back to `/`). The first sign-in shows a consent screen once, the owning team included. It replaces a project account's sign-in, on the project and, started in a sandbox, the sandbox's own too.
- `logout?return=<path>`: ends the visitor's sign-in on the project; they stay signed in to yard, and a team member keeps team access. In a sandbox it ends the sign-ins that count there: the sandbox's own, and a yard account's on the project.
- `email` and `email/verify`: the sign-in page's two calls for project accounts, `POST` with a JSON body, answered only for the project's own pages (Project accounts below; the bodies and every refusal are in the sign-in page docs).
- `app/start`, `app` and `app/token`: how a project's own app (desktop or mobile) signs a user in with a project account (Project accounts below; the bodies and every answer are in the sign-in page docs, section Signing in from your own app).
- `me`: always `200`: `{ authenticated: true, user_id, email, username?, avatar_url?, entitlement, tier?, tier_key?, holding }` or exactly `{ authenticated: false, entitlement: "none" }`. `holding` is what the visitor holds themselves, as `window.yard.ownership()` reports it (`owned`, `is_trial`, `trial_expires_at`, `trial_used`, `is_subscription`, `transaction_id`, `tier_id`, `tier_key`, `tier_name`): a team member's `entitlement` is `team` while `holding` says what they bought.
- `guest`: a stable identity for signed-out visitors (guest play). `POST` issues the browser a guest id (kept 400 days), `GET` answers `{ guest: true|false }`, `DELETE` forgets it; the id itself is never returned. From then on every request from the project's own pages, WebSocket upgrades included, carries `X-Yard-Guest-Id`; only the project's pages may issue or forget one (`403 cross_origin`). After a guest signs in both ids arrive: move the guest's rows to the user, then `DELETE`. Never mint your own token.
- `products` (GET) and `fulfillments/{transaction_id}` (POST): the visitor's product holdings and consumable delivery (products.md).

A session and a team member's team access each cover the landing page, every service and every sandbox of one project on one host (the `yard.sh` address and a custom domain each sign in separately); a project account's session is narrower, covering the project alone or the one sandbox it was made in. Page scripts cannot read either, and both count only for requests from the project's own pages and for top-level navigations: a fetch, form post or WebSocket from a page on another host arrives signed out. Never change state on `GET`, since a link from elsewhere still arrives signed in.

## External apps

Software outside the project (desktop, mobile, a backend elsewhere) signs users in with the project as a standard OpenID Connect application (issuer, client, redirect URIs and claims: the docs page above, section External apps). The sign-in must ask for the `yard_account` scope (and `email` for the address), or the endpoints answer `403 insufficient_scope`. Every call also names the app it is made for, by its client ID, and is answered only for a token that was issued to that app, so a server can trust the answer for a token a client handed it; a call that names no app is refused. The access token identifies one user of one project and reaches exactly three endpoints: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/api/yard-auth/get-userinfo.md (identity and entitlement; call it on every launch, since purchase status is not in the ID token; its ids and the ID token's `sub` are the project's id for the person, the same value as `X-Yard-User-Id`), https://docs.yard.sh/v1/api/yard-auth/get-user-products.md and https://docs.yard.sh/v1/api/yard-auth/fulfill-user-purchase.md. Redirect URI origins also count as checkout `return_to` origins (checkout.md).

## Project accounts

Project accounts are accounts your users create in your project, beside yard accounts (a person's own account at yard, usable on any project). A project can have users of both kinds; a user is one or the other and never changes kind. They are turned on per project with the Project Accounts switch on the project's Authentication page, in the dashboard only, and need Pro (`.team_permissions.project_accounts`) and email sending ready on the project's custom domain. Read whether the switch is on from `project_accounts_enabled` (`yard projects show`, MCP `get_project`). No command, MCP tool or API call changes it, so never try to turn them on: tell the user where the switch is. The sign-in page's path is `auth.sign_in_path` in settings.json (project-files.md): every link the project mails lands there, so without it no link can be mailed. Docs: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/yard-auth.md, section Project accounts.

Build a sign-in page only when the project has project accounts on and its settings.json names `auth.sign_in_path`. Start from the whole page in https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/sign-in-page.md rather than writing one from memory.

- What it does: a signed-out visitor is sent to the page (the gate, below); the person types an address and the page posts it; yard mails a link from the project's domain that opens the same page with the token in the URL fragment; yard's script on the page takes the token off the address bar before the page's own scripts run and posts it, and the page goes to the path yard answers. The first link followed for an address makes its account.
- The mail is yard's own unless the team saves its own on the Sign-In Email row of the project's Authentication page, in the dashboard only (Pro, a role that can make changes): a subject, HTML and optional plain text, naming only `{{link}}`, `{{project_name}}`, `{{host}}` and `{{expires_in}}`. `{{link}}` must be in the HTML (and in the plain text when there is some) and never in the subject, images go by full `https` address to a file in a public service, and a save is live at once for the project and its sandboxes. No command, MCP tool or API call reads or sets it, so draft the HTML for the user to paste there. A yard account always gets yard's own mail. Docs: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/yard-auth.md, section Your own sign-in email.
- The gate: while project accounts are on, a signed-out navigation to an `authenticated` or `users` service lands on the page with `?return=`, the path asked for under the project's root (or the sandbox's) with its query, which is the `return` the sign-in calls take. A draft, a private sandbox and a `team` service always use yard's sign-in, and so does a project or sandbox whose release names no page, or whose page falls under a deployed service that needs sign-in, or under none. The gate goes by the deployed services, so until the page's own service is deployed its path falls under whichever deployed service covers it; when that is a public one, often the landing page, visitors are sent to the path and that service answers it, likely with not found. For about 15 seconds after a return from yard's sign-in, a visitor still signed out gets a short Try again page instead, so never send visitors on to Sign in with yard without a click. Turning project accounts off, or leaving Pro, sends visitors to yard's sign-in again within about a minute.
- Where it sits: the page must fall under a `public` service, the one whose `url` is the longest match for its path (no `url` means `/<name>`). A site that all needs sign-in adds a small public service at the page's path holding the page and its files. A settings.json push or save that puts the page under a service that needs sign-in is refused with `sign_in_page_not_public` and the reason, and so are a GitHub sync and a publish, whether project accounts are on or not; `yard dev` and `yard service check` warn first. A public service with no files yet is accepted; until it is deployed, the gate goes by whichever deployed service covers the path (above). Changing one service on its own (its access, its `url`, deleting it) through the API or the MCP tools is not checked, so on a published release check the layout yourself before such a change.
- The helpers: while project accounts are on, yard adds `embed.js` and the project's data to the sign-in page in whichever service it is, at the address `auth.sign_in_path` names only (not at `/login/index.html` or another path of the same app). Build the page on `window.yard.signIn(email)`, whose `return` defaults to the page's own `?return=`, `window.yard.linkSignIn()`, and `window.yard.signInWithYardURL()` for a Sign in with yard button (landing-pages.md). The script has already taken and posted the link's token, so never read it from `location.hash` while `window.yard` exists. Only a page whose own `Content-Security-Policy` keeps the script out (it loads from `https://yard.sh`; allowing that in `script-src` is the simpler fix) uses `fetch`, and it reads the fragment only when `window.yard` is absent.
- Asking for a link never says whether an address has an account, or which kind, so the page always says "check your inbox". An address that is a yard account's, where that account has signed in to the project, gets yard's own mail and comes back as that yard account; a project that already has yard account users keeps a Sign in with yard button (a link to `__yard/auth/login`, built with `signInWithYardURL()`) for people whose yard address is not one they would type.
- A link opened in a browser that did not ask for it asks to confirm, naming the address: show the address and confirm only from a click, never automatically. A confirm click within a second of the page coming to the front (loading, the window gaining focus, the page becoming visible) is refused as `click_required` with nothing posted, so keep the confirm step showing and let the person click again; a page built with `fetch` ignores such a click itself.
- The page holds the link's token for a moment: keep other parties' scripts (analytics, chat, tag managers) off it, and never show it in a frame (the page refuses to run inside one). While project accounts are on, the edge also answers `frame-ancestors 'self'` for the whole service that holds the page, so a sign-in page in the landing page's service keeps the landing page out of other sites' frames too; a small service of its own avoids that.
- Test with another address: the developer's own is a yard account that has signed in to the project, so it gets yard's mail, not the project's. Use an alias such as `you+test@example.com`. Signing in as a test address leaves team access, and Sign in with yard replaces it.
- On a draft or a private sandbox both calls need team access, like the page itself; a teammate signed out of the project is still let in by team access and, at a service that needs sign-in, lands on the page inside the draft to test from there. Each sandbox has its own project accounts and its own sign-in, and its visitors are sent to the page its own release names. `yard dev` prints the link instead of mailing it, and gates the same way (local-dev.md).
- From the project's own app: an app can't use the emailed link itself, since the link signs in the browser that follows it. Instead the app asks with the address the person typed and shows them a 9-digit code; yard sends the usual sign-in mail; the person signs in on the site as always and is then asked for the app's code; the app, which has been asking every few seconds, collects a sign-in for that user that lasts 30 days and sends it as the project's session cookie on its calls to the project's own services, which see the user as they see any signed-in visitor. The code is typed, never put in a link, and works once, for 15 minutes, only for the address the app asked for. The code is typed on a plain page yard draws, or on a page of the team's own when settings.json names `auth.app_sign_in_path` (project-files.md); that page checks the code with `window.yard.appSignIn(code)` where yard adds `embed.js` (the sign-in page and the landing page's service), and with one `fetch` elsewhere, so the simplest place for it is the sign-in page itself. It is on wherever project accounts are on, with the email call's limits. `yard dev` stands in for it (local-dev.md). Not yet: a team member's own app sign-in is seen as nobody, and the app gets a session, not OpenID tokens (External apps above still sign in yard accounts only). Docs: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/sign-in-page.md, section Signing in from your own app.
- Checkout and gifts send visitors to the page too: Log in on yard's checkout page and Claim on yard's gift page bring a signed-out visitor there with a `return` naming a `__yard/checkout` path, which takes them back to checkout once signed in. The helpers pass it on; a page built with `fetch` must pass it unchanged too. Buying with a project account, and what the project sees after: checkout.md.
- yard mails a project account about its own purchases (the receipt, a refund, a renewal that failed, a trial that is ending), each saying where to manage it: at yard.sh, signed in with that address. When a project it holds leaves pre-order or early access, it gets yard's mail that the project is now available, as yard accounts do; each such mail to a project account carries a "Stop these emails" link that turns that mail off for the address.
- Delete, on a project account's sheet on the dashboard's Users page (no command, MCP tool or API key deletes one: tell the user where it is), signs the person out everywhere; the address can sign up again, as a new user with a new id. A yard account has no Delete. When the account bought, yard keeps the sale's record and the dialog says so first: its subscriptions (product subscriptions included) and trials end at once and the unused part of a paid period is refunded (a payment that lands later is refunded in full), yard emails the buyer, what they bought once stays theirs at yard.sh under the receipt's address, and its sales stay in the team's transactions with that address, marked `user_deleted` under the same `user_id`. Each subscription and trial it ends fires its events, a subscription's with `cancel_reason` `account_deleted`; the partial refund of a period's unused part sends no refund event (it shows on the sale), while a payment that lands after the delete is refunded in full and sends one with `refund_reason` `account_deleted`. A subscription the team gave it (products.md) ends too, as a bought one does, with nothing to refund, and what it was given once stays its at yard.sh like what it bought. In a sandbox nothing is charged, refunded or mailed. Docs: https://docs.yard.sh/v1/platform/services/yard-auth.md, section Deleting a project account.
